## Theme

Passages and tunnels hacked out of raw rock, somewhere under what used to be a city.
Not a designed dungeon — a sprawl. Corridors that widen into chambers for no reason
and narrow again just as arbitrarily. Tool marks on the walls, though nothing says
what made them or how recently. Dust, grit, cold stone, and the particular silence of
being very far underground.

This is the first floor, and it is a filter. It exists to thin the herd. Most of what
you meet here is survivable; the danger is in volume, exhaustion, and the crawlers who
assume survivable means safe.

The tone is dry and matter-of-fact, with the underlying absurdity left to speak for
itself. Describe what is there. Don't editorialize, don't welcome the crawler, don't
perform — the place is strange enough without help.

The one thing that isn't ordinary rock: this is being broadcast. Not overtly, not yet.
A camera-drone drifting past and pretending it wasn't. A chamber that is a little too
well lit for somewhere with no light source. Faded sponsor stencilling on stone that
predates any sponsor. Signs that the space has been arranged for viewing. Use these
sparingly — perhaps one room in six. They should read as unsettling rather than funny.

## Denizens

Low-level, in numbers, in neighborhoods — the same kind of thing tends to occupy a
stretch of tunnel together, so a crawler learns the local hazard and then leaves it
behind. Riff freely within that:

- Goblins with scavenged tools, territorial and better organized than they look.
- Slimes in the wet stretches, slow and patient and genuinely dangerous to the careless.
- Things that have adapted to the dark and would rather not be looked at directly.
- Something occasionally far out of place here, well beyond what floor one should hold.
  Rare. Not a mistake when it happens.

## Set Pieces

- Chambers that were obviously *arranged* — furniture, a table set for a meal, a room
  that has been staged rather than found.
- Old crawler remains, and the marks people leave when they think they might not be back.
- Shafts, drops, and openings that clearly go somewhere and are not the stairs.
- Tool marks that stop mid-stroke, as though whatever was digging simply stopped.

## Loot Flavor

Scavenged and improvised, mostly: tools pressed into service as weapons, padding worn
as armor, things taken off crawlers who no longer need them. Occasionally something
that does not belong down here at all — clean, precise, clearly manufactured — with no
explanation offered for how it arrived.
